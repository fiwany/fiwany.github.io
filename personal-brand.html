Personal Brand

I wanted to start freelancing, so I built my own brand to show clients how I design.

Outcome

Won my first freelance client, a matcha brand, after he saw my brand work.

The problem

I wanted to freelance, but had no client work to show yet.

I was pivoting into design and took a graphic communications course to explore it. When the final project asked us to create a brand, I made one for myself, so I could show clients how I design before anyone had hired me.

Defining the direction

Three keywords guided every decision.

Impactful

Bold, straightforward, attention-grabbing

Intentional

Grounded in meaning, restrained

Experimental

Edgy, adaptive, out of the box

Initial moodboards

1More polished, but willing to experiment a little: standard fonts that play with texture

2Polished, impactful, but commercial

3Hand-drawn and organic, but more childlike

4Still hand-drawn, but slightly more serious

Final moodboard

Slightly vintage and artistic, but still intentionally modern: contrasting typefaces, white space and shapes for the logo, and organic shapes for key visuals.

Logo, key visuals & color

Logo development

Using my full name, Fiwany, allowed me to experiment. The bigger W, A, and Y spell “way,” for bringing a client’s project to the finish line.

Color

My first drafts only used red, blue and brown on grey. I added yellow for wind later, turning it into a triad for the elements, with beige and brown as bases.

Sticker printing

Brand in use

Business cards with my logo on the front and my name on the back, and the final A5 sticker sheet.

What came next

After seeing my brand work, my first freelance client agreed to work with me on his matcha brand. People who see my branded items often ask where they’re from.

Reflection

My first drafts only used red, blue and brown. I almost left out the yellow because it looked too Bauhaus, but my teacher showed me how the context of my design gave it a new meaning. Now I check every design against my keywords before finalizing.
